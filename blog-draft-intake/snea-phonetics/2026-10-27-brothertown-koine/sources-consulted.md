# Sources consulted — comparable-project standard practices (2026-10-27)

## 1. Wôpanâak Language Reclamation Project — documentary basis and method

**Verdict: confirmed, with one date correction.**

- Documentary basis (WLRP site): "In addition to the 'Eliot Indian Bible,' many
  handwritten deeds, wills, petitions to the Massachusett Bay Colony and Plymouth
  Colony… signed by Wampanoag citizens and written in the Wôpanâak language, have
  been preserved."
- Sister-language comparison (WLRP site): "sound change comparisons with other
  sister languages… to ensure a conservative linguistic foundation"; MIT: "combining
  careful study of the available texts with cross-linguistic comparison within the
  Algonquian family."
- Dormancy: "no speakers in over a century" (MIT); "after more than 150 years of
  dormancy… without regular communication with Wôpanâak for six generations" (WLRP);
  no record of the language spoken after 1833 (The Crimson, 2024).
- **Correction:** first new native speaker is **Mae Baird, born before July 4, 2004**
  (The Independent) — not 2017–2019; that window is the immersion-school/press wave
  (AP, Nov 2017: 19 children in the immersion program).
- Confidence: **high** — project site + university partner + multiple dated press
  accounts agree.

## 2. Kaurna revival (KWP/KWK, Adelaide) — uniform dialect, open coinage

**Verdict: confirmed, with one count discrepancy flagged.**

- Uniform dialect: "KWP has created a uniform dialect of the language" (Wikipedia,
  Kaurna language; backed there by the Teichelmann & Schürmann 1840s record).
- Open coinage with elders (ABC News, 4 Aug 2022): "The dictionary includes 4,000
  new words created in consultation with local elders and Kaurna speakers."
- Comparative calibration (Rob Amery, in the ABC piece): "detailed comparative work
  with neighbouring languages so that we can best work out the optimum pronunciation
  of those words."
- **Discrepancy (unresolved between sources):** ABC and Wikipedia report ~4,000 new
  words; the foundation's own materials describe **400 new-and-repurposed** entries.
  The article therefore cites the practice, not the figure. Confidence: **high** on
  the practice; **medium** on any count.
- Community gatekeeping: "This process is overseen by a panel of language experts
  from the Kaurna community" (kaurnawarra.org.au/kaurna-language-requests).

## 3. Cornish Standard Written Form (2008) — public adjudication of contested orthographies

**Verdict: confirmed.**

- SWF agreement (Cornish Language Partnership): "an agreement on a Standard Written
  Form was reached… ratified by the Partnership on 9th May 2008"; "after two years
  of negotiations" (Wikipedia, Standard Written Form).
- Public contestation (MAGA Kernow): open conferences (Sept 2006, Oct 2007), a
  foreign "commission of eminent language planners," published position statements
  still downloadable.
- Principled base-variety choice (SWF specification): "The orthography as a whole
  leans toward a Middle Cornish base, since in many cases the correct RLC or TC
  pronunciation can be deduced from an RMC form, but not vice versa."
- Nuance: the commission recommended "a consensus approach… instead of choosing one
  of the existing forms" — the SWF was a negotiated blend, publicly argued.
- Confidence: **high.**

Sources: https://www.magakernow.org.uk/default_page-346-lang-en-gb.html ·
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Written_Form ·
https://www.kernowek.net/Specification_Final_Version.pdf

## 4. Marking generated forms; selecting variants rather than averaging

**Verdict: confirmed for both.**

- **Marking generated forms** (Mutsun dictionary, Warner/Butler/Geary 2016, LD&C
  Special Publication No. 11, open access): "We have made up words for some of these
  things, and… we have included them in the dictionary. These are marked 'New word
  made from' so that you can tell they were not part of how Mutsun was spoken in the
  past." Attestation flags in entries: "[Attested only once]". Example sentences all
  "from real Mutsun texts… not modern made-up sentences."
- **Selection over averaging:**
  - palawa kani (Tasmanian Aboriginal Centre): "the value of any recording is only
    as credible as the person who wrote it down"; early program work "determine[d]
    the most accurate, hence most reliable, recorders"; explicit rejection of picking
    "the easiest to say or the one with the prettiest or most fanciful translation."
  - Mutsun learner guidance between attested rivals: "So use *taprey*. It's the
    normal way to say 'above' in Mutsun."
  - Mutsun transcription policy: "We follow Harrington wherever possible" — one
    recorded tradition privileged over another on evidence.
  - Cornish SWF: principled base variety (above), not equal blending — and the
    attempted equal standing of two attested varieties "has been hard to maintain"
    (Davies-Deacon 2020, QUB).
- Confidence: **high** on marking; **medium-high** on selection-as-principle
  (direct quotes exist; the practice is rarely named under a single rubric).
