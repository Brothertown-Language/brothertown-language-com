# Image recommendations — "Where the words come from"

Recommendations, best-fit first. Each candidate is a faithful reproduction of a
public-domain historical work (or a CC0-released institution scan) — safe to
publish with a credit line; every published image carries its license credit.

## 1. Primary recommendation — *A Key into the Language of America* (1643), title page

- **Fit**: this is the derivation's primary conversational record and the source of
  the article's calibration anchors (⟨nohsh⟩ 'my father', ⟨awán⟩ 'who', ⟨mutáh⟩
  'heart') — the first work of Algonquian linguistics published in English, the
  literal answer to the title's question: where the words come from.
- **Source**: Wikimedia Commons,
  `File:A Key into the Language of America.jpg` (title page of a first edition,
  London: Gregory Dexter, 1643).
- **License**: Creative Commons **Public Domain Mark 1.0** (faithful photographic
  reproduction of a 2-D public-domain work); full-resolution 2,053 × 3,200.
- **Alt-candidates**: full 1643 scan as a Commons PDF (IA early-english-books
  mirror) or the same title page scanned from an Internet Archive / 1866
  Massachusetts Historical Society reprint copy, if an in-house render at the
  corpus's raster standards is preferred.

## 2. Alternative — Samson Occom mezzotint (Spilsbury after Chamberlin, 1768)

- **Fit**: humanizes the founding paragraph — the article's opening names Samson
  Occom's leadership of the 1773/1785 founding.
- **Source**: Wikimedia Commons, `File:Samson Occom by Jonathan Spilsbury, after
  Mason Chamberlin, 1768… - NPG-NPG 71 15Occom1L-000001.jpg` (National Portrait
  Gallery; category "Samson Occom").
- **License**: the NPG explicitly released this scan under **CC0**; the
  underlying 1768 mezzotint is public domain.
- **Note**: if the site already carries an Occom image, this slot goes to the
  third candidate instead; do not duplicate portraits across posts.

## 3. Alternative — Eliot Indian Bible (1663), title page

- **Fit**: ties the shared-spine evidence and the Wôpanâak paragraph together —
  the Eliot corpus is the largest single Massachusett record and the trunk of
  modern Wôpanâak's reclamation.
- **Source**: Wikimedia Commons, `File:John Elliot Bible, 1663.jpg` (Houghton
  Library copy).
- **License**: **PD-old / CC-PD-Mark** (1663 imprint; faithful reproduction).
- **In-house alternative**: the corpus repository stages the 1661/1663 volumes —
  a title-page render at native scan resolution is producible without acquisition.

## Sourcing guidance (applies to all future drafts)

- **Candidate sites for PD/CC0/CC-BY imagery**: Wikimedia Commons (the default —
  rich PD-New-England historical material), Openclipart (CC0 — decorative/graphic
  devices only; no historical content there), Flickr Commons, Library of Congress,
  Internet Archive / HathiTrust (public-domain books and engravings), and the
  project's own tracked public-domain page scans.
- **Exclusions, enforced**: the tracked proofing renders of in-copyright sources
  (the C-number page renders for Fielding 2005, Rudes 1997, and similar) are
  quoted-page evidence for the scholarly register — never blog images. Material
  owned or gated by living communities (e.g. WLRP's vocabulary and imagery) is
  not sourced without permission.
- **Credit rule**: every published image carries its source site and license
  (e.g. "via Wikimedia Commons, Public Domain Mark 1.0"), and the file lands in
  this draft's `images/` folder only after its license note is recorded here.
